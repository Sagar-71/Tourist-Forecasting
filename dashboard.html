Time-series forecasting · 2016–2024

Forecasting India's monthly tourist arrivals

Official arrival figures are published about three weeks after the month ends. Seven models were trained to estimate the number sooner, from past arrivals and live Google search interest — then tested month by month across the last three years.

108 months 7 models 36 test months 25 features walk-forward validation

What was achieved

Exponential smoothing forecasts next month's arrivals to within 11% on average — 89.0% accuracy against 61.6% for the seasonal benchmark it has to beat. Every model tested cleared that benchmark; the neural network did not win.

The series being forecast

Nine years of monthly arrivals. Strong seasonality — December peaks, summer troughs — interrupted by a collapse to 2,820 arrivals in April 2020, down from 1.23 million four months earlier. That break is what makes this series hard, and why the test window sits after it.

How it was tested

Walk-forward validation. For each month in the test window the model is retrained from scratch on everything before it, then asked for one month ahead. The training set grows by one month at each step.

Why not a random split

A random train/test split would train on months that come after the one being predicted. The model would be learning from the future, and the score would be flattering and meaningless.

What the model may see

Arrivals up to the previous month, since figures are published about three weeks late, plus the current month's search interest, which is available immediately.

Why the benchmark matters

Seasonal naive just repeats last year's value for the same month. A forecast that cannot beat it has not earned its complexity. All six real models do.

Questions this raises

Why doesn't the LSTM win?

There are about 100 monthly observations. A recurrent network has more parameters than that can identify, so it overfits where a seasonal model generalises. It was run to check rather than to assume — it lands mid-table, with a validation loss of 0.0059 on scaled data.

Did Google search interest actually help?

Only marginally. Because arrivals are published just three weeks late, last month's real figure is already available when the forecast is made, and it carries most of the signal search interest would otherwise supply. Search would matter more if the publication lag were longer.

Why does the test window start in 2022?

It is the most recent three years, and it sits after the COVID collapse. Percentage error across April 2020 is meaningless — any miss against 2,820 arrivals is enormous in relative terms.

What are the limits?

108 months is short for monthly seasonal modelling — nine cycles, two of them disrupted. And the test window is entirely post-COVID recovery, so these numbers describe a recovering market, not a steady one.
